# Microsoft Q2 FY2026: Cloud Crosses $50B, But Azure Growth Concerns Linger

Microsoft delivered a strong Q2 FY2026, beating Wall Street expectations across revenue, operating income, and earnings per share. Microsoft Cloud revenue crossed $50 billion for the first time, and Azure grew 39% year-over-year. But the market focused on what's coming next: Azure growth is expected to decelerate in Q3 due to capacity constraints, and Microsoft's AI infrastructure spending is accelerating faster than revenue can keep up.

Here's what happened.

## The Numbers: A Beat Across the Board

- **Revenue:** $81.3 billion, up 17% year-over-year (15% in constant currency) — beat the $80.3 billion expected
- **GAAP EPS:** $5.16, up 60% year-over-year
- **Non-GAAP EPS:** $4.14, up 24% year-over-year (21% in constant currency) — beat the $4.09-4.11 expected
- **Operating Income:** $38.3 billion, up 21% year-over-year (19% in constant currency)
- **Microsoft Cloud Revenue:** $51.5 billion, up 26% year-over-year (24% in constant currency)

*Translation: Microsoft crushed estimates. Non-GAAP EPS strips out the impact of Microsoft's investment in OpenAI, which distorts the GAAP numbers. The $4.14 non-GAAP EPS is the cleaner number to focus on — it shows the underlying business performance without the accounting noise from the OpenAI stake.*

## Microsoft Cloud: The $50 Billion Milestone

Microsoft Cloud revenue hit $51.5 billion this quarter, crossing the $50 billion mark for the first time. This includes Azure, Microsoft 365 Commercial, Dynamics 365, and other cloud services.

CFO Amy Hood said: "Microsoft Cloud revenue crossed $50 billion this quarter, reflecting the strong demand for our portfolio of services."

The commercial remaining performance obligation (cRPO) — essentially the backlog of future cloud revenue already contracted — grew 110% to $625 billion. That's a massive number, and it tells you enterprises are signing long-term cloud deals with Microsoft at an accelerating pace.

*Translation: "Remaining performance obligation" is the total value of contracts Microsoft has already signed but hasn't yet delivered. Think of it as future guaranteed revenue. A 110% increase means companies are committing to Microsoft's cloud services years in advance, which gives Microsoft high visibility into future revenue.*

## Azure: Growing Fast, But Capacity Constraints Are Real

Azure and other cloud services revenue grew 39% year-over-year (38% in constant currency). That's strong growth, but it's decelerating from previous quarters.

The bigger issue is what Microsoft said about Q3: **Azure growth is expected to slow further** due to capacity constraints. Microsoft is spending heavily to build out AI infrastructure, but demand is outpacing supply. The company can't add data center capacity fast enough to meet customer demand for AI workloads.

*Translation: "Capacity constraints" means Microsoft doesn't have enough data center space, servers, or GPUs to handle all the AI demand from customers. This is a good problem to have (demand exceeds supply), but it means Microsoft is leaving revenue on the table in the short term. The company is racing to build more capacity, but that takes time.*

Microsoft's capital expenditures (CapEx) in Q2 were $15.8 billion, and the company said full-year FY2026 CapEx will be "slightly higher than $80 billion." Most of that is going toward AI infrastructure.

*Translation: Capital expenditures (CapEx) are the money a company spends on long-term assets like buildings, servers, and data centers. Microsoft is spending over $80 billion this year to build out its AI infrastructure. That's more than the entire annual revenue of most Fortune 500 companies. The bet is that this spending will pay off in future AI revenue, but it's a massive upfront investment.*

## The OpenAI Accounting Distortion

Microsoft's GAAP results include the impact of its investment in OpenAI, which creates a huge distortion in the numbers.

- **Q2 FY2026 GAAP Net Income:** $38.5 billion (up 60%)
- **Q2 FY2026 Non-GAAP Net Income:** $30.9 billion (up 23%)
- **OpenAI Impact:** Reduced GAAP net income by $7.6 billion, or $1.02 per share

*Translation: Microsoft owns a significant stake in OpenAI (the company behind ChatGPT). Under accounting rules, Microsoft has to "mark to market" this investment every quarter, meaning it adjusts the value based on OpenAI's latest valuation. In Q2, OpenAI's valuation increased dramatically, which added $7.6 billion to Microsoft's GAAP net income. But this isn't cash — it's just an accounting entry. That's why the non-GAAP number (which excludes this) is more useful for understanding the actual business performance.*

## Productivity & Business Processes: Microsoft 365 Keeps Growing

Revenue in this segment was $34.1 billion, up 16% year-over-year (14% in constant currency).

- **Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud:** +17% (14% constant currency)
- **Microsoft 365 Consumer cloud:** +29% (27% constant currency)
- **LinkedIn:** +11% (10% constant currency)

Microsoft 365 Copilot (the AI assistant built into Office apps) is still in early stages, but Microsoft said it's seeing strong adoption among enterprise customers. The company doesn't break out Copilot revenue separately yet, but it's clear this is a major growth driver going forward.

*Translation: Microsoft 365 Copilot is an AI assistant that helps you write emails, summarize documents, and analyze data in Excel. It costs $30 per user per month on top of the regular Microsoft 365 subscription. Enterprises are starting to roll it out, and if adoption accelerates, this could add billions in high-margin revenue.*

## More Personal Computing: Windows and Xbox Steady

Revenue in this segment was $14.3 billion, up 3% year-over-year (2% in constant currency).

- **Windows OEM:** +6% (PC manufacturers are selling more devices)
- **Xbox content and services:** +6% (5% constant currency)
- **Search and news advertising (ex-TAC):** +16% (15% constant currency)

This segment is the slowest-growing part of Microsoft's business, but it's stable and profitable. Windows and Xbox aren't growth engines anymore, but they generate steady cash flow.

## What's Coming in Q3 FY2026

Microsoft guided for Q3 FY2026 revenue of $68.8 billion to $69.8 billion (midpoint ~$69.3 billion), slightly below the $69.8 billion analysts expected.

The key concern: **Azure growth is expected to decelerate in Q3** due to capacity constraints. Microsoft is building out more data centers, but it takes time. The company said it expects Azure growth to reaccelerate in the second half of FY2026 as new capacity comes online.

*Translation: Microsoft is essentially saying: "We could grow Azure faster, but we're supply-constrained. Give us a few quarters to build more data centers, and growth will pick back up." Investors don't like hearing "we're leaving revenue on the table," even if it's temporary. That's why the guidance disappointed.*

## The Bottom Line for Investors

Microsoft is in a unique position: it's the leader in enterprise cloud and AI, but it's spending so much on infrastructure that margins are under pressure. The company is betting that today's $80 billion+ CapEx will generate massive AI revenue in the future, but that payoff is still uncertain.

The bull case is that Microsoft is building an unassailable lead in enterprise AI. Azure is the default cloud for most Fortune 500 companies, Microsoft 365 Copilot is embedding AI into every office worker's workflow, and the $625 billion backlog shows enterprises are committing long-term. If the AI infrastructure spending pays off, Microsoft's moat gets even wider.

The bear case is that Microsoft is spending $80 billion a year on AI infrastructure without clear proof that it will generate proportional returns. Azure growth is decelerating due to capacity constraints, which means Microsoft is supply-constrained in the near term. And at some point, investors will demand to see AI revenue growth match the infrastructure spending.

The question isn't whether Microsoft is winning in AI — it clearly is. The question is whether the economics of AI will be as profitable as the cloud business that came before it.
