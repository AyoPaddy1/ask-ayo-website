# Meta Q4 2025: Record Revenue, But Reality Labs Losses Deepen

Meta delivered a strong Q4 2025, beating Wall Street expectations with $59.9 billion in revenue and earnings per share of $8.88. The Family of Apps business (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger) continues to grow, with 3.58 billion daily active people and advertising revenue up 24%. But the Reality Labs division — Meta's bet on virtual and augmented reality — lost $6 billion in the quarter, and the company is guiding for $115 billion to $135 billion in capital expenditures in 2026, mostly for AI infrastructure.

Here's what happened.

## The Numbers: A Beat Across the Board

- **Revenue:** $59.89 billion, up 24% year-over-year (23% in constant currency) — beat the $58.42 billion expected
- **EPS:** $8.88 (non-GAAP), up 11% year-over-year — beat the $8.20-8.25 expected
- **Operating Income:** $24.75 billion, up 6% year-over-year
- **Net Income:** $22.77 billion, up 9% year-over-year
- **Costs and Expenses:** $35.15 billion, up 40% year-over-year

*Translation: Meta crushed estimates on both revenue and earnings. The 40% jump in costs and expenses is the eye-catching number — that's Meta spending heavily on AI infrastructure, data centers, and technical talent. The bet is that this spending will pay off in future AI revenue, but it's a massive upfront investment that's eating into margins.*

## Family of Apps: The Cash Machine

The Family of Apps segment (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger) generated $58.94 billion in revenue, up 25% year-over-year. This is where Meta makes almost all of its money.

- **Advertising Revenue:** $58.14 billion, up 24% year-over-year
- **Family Daily Active People (DAP):** 3.58 billion, up 7% year-over-year
- **Ad Impressions:** +18% year-over-year
- **Average Price Per Ad:** +6% year-over-year

*Translation: "Daily active people" (DAP) measures how many unique users open at least one of Meta's apps (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger) on a given day. 3.58 billion people is nearly half the world's population. The fact that Meta is still growing this number by 7% year-over-year tells you the company isn't saturated yet.*

The advertising business is firing on all cylinders. Ad impressions (the number of ads shown) grew 18%, and the average price per ad grew 6%. That's the ideal mix: more ads being shown, and advertisers paying more for each one.

*Translation: Ad impressions growing faster than price per ad means Meta is showing more ads without annoying users enough to leave. If price per ad were growing faster, it would suggest Meta is running out of ad inventory and having to charge more for the same space. The current mix is healthier.*

## Reality Labs: The $6 Billion Quarterly Loss

Reality Labs — Meta's division focused on virtual reality (Quest headsets), augmented reality (smart glasses), and the metaverse — lost $6.02 billion in Q4, up from a $4.97 billion loss in Q4 2024.

For the full year 2025, Reality Labs lost $19.19 billion, up from $17.73 billion in 2024.

Revenue for Reality Labs was $955 million in Q4, down 12% year-over-year. That's a tiny fraction of Meta's total revenue, and it's shrinking.

*Translation: Reality Labs is Meta's long-term bet on the next computing platform. Mark Zuckerberg believes AR/VR will eventually replace smartphones, but the market isn't there yet. The division is burning $19 billion a year, and revenue is declining. This is a classic "invest now, profit later" bet — except it's been "later" for years, and the losses keep growing.*

Meta said Reality Labs operating losses are expected to "remain similar to 2025 levels" in 2026. That means another ~$19 billion in losses next year.

## The AI Infrastructure Spending Surge

Meta's costs and expenses jumped 40% year-over-year to $35.15 billion in Q4. The biggest drivers:

**Infrastructure costs:** Third-party cloud spend, higher depreciation, and higher infrastructure operating expenses. Meta is building out massive AI data centers to train and run its AI models.

**Employee compensation:** Meta ended Q4 with 78,800 employees, up 6% year-over-year. The company is hiring technical talent for AI and monetization.

*Translation: "Depreciation" is the accounting term for spreading the cost of long-term assets (like servers and data centers) over their useful life. When Meta spends billions on AI infrastructure, it doesn't expense it all at once — it depreciates it over several years. Higher depreciation means Meta has been spending heavily on infrastructure in recent years, and those costs are now flowing through the income statement.*

For 2026, Meta is guiding for:

- **Total Expenses:** $162 billion to $169 billion (midpoint $165.5 billion)
- **Capital Expenditures:** $115 billion to $135 billion (midpoint $125 billion)

Most of the CapEx is going toward "Meta Superintelligence Labs" — Meta's internal name for its AI research and infrastructure efforts.

*Translation: Capital expenditures (CapEx) are the money a company spends on long-term assets like buildings, servers, and data centers. Meta is guiding for up to $135 billion in CapEx in 2026 — more than the entire annual revenue of most Fortune 500 companies. This is one of the largest infrastructure buildouts in corporate history, rivaling what Microsoft and Amazon are spending on AI.*

Despite this massive spending, Meta said it expects 2026 operating income to be **above 2025 levels**. That's a bold claim, and it suggests Meta believes the AI investments will start paying off in revenue growth.

## What's Coming in Q1 2026

Meta guided for Q1 2026 revenue of $53.5 billion to $56.5 billion (midpoint $55 billion), well above the $51.41 billion analysts expected.

*Translation: Strong guidance is a signal that Meta sees momentum continuing into 2026. The fact that the guidance midpoint is $3.6 billion above expectations tells you Meta is confident in its advertising business and AI-driven ad targeting improvements.*

## The Bottom Line for Investors

Meta is in a unique position: it has a wildly profitable advertising business (Family of Apps) that's still growing, but it's spending unprecedented amounts on two long-term bets — AI infrastructure and Reality Labs — that haven't yet proven they'll generate proportional returns.

The bull case is that Meta's AI investments will dramatically improve ad targeting and create new revenue streams (like AI assistants, enterprise AI tools, and AI-generated content). The Family of Apps business is a cash machine with 3.58 billion daily users, and Meta is using that cash to build an AI moat. If the AI spending pays off, Meta's dominance in social media and digital advertising gets even stronger.

The bear case is that Meta is spending $125 billion a year on AI infrastructure without clear proof that it will generate proportional returns. Reality Labs is burning $19 billion a year with no path to profitability in sight. And at some point, investors will demand to see AI revenue growth match the infrastructure spending. The 40% jump in costs and expenses is eating into margins, and if revenue growth slows, the profitability picture could deteriorate quickly.

The question isn't whether Meta is winning in social media — it clearly is. The question is whether the AI and metaverse bets will pay off before they consume too much of the cash flow from the core business.
