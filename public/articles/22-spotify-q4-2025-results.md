# Spotify Q4 2025 Earnings: Record Users, Record Profits

Spotify closed out 2025 with its best quarter ever. The company added more users than in any quarter in its history, hit record profit margins, and delivered earnings per share that came in well above expectations. For a company that spent years losing money, this is a turning point.

Here's what happened.

## The Numbers: Everything Beat Expectations

- **Revenue:** €4.53 billion ($4.83 billion), up 7% year-over-year (13% on a constant-currency basis)
- **Net Income:** €1.17 billion — more than tripled from €367 million a year ago
- **EPS:** €4.43 vs. €2.74 expected
- **Gross Margin:** 33.1% — a record high for the company
- **Operating Income:** €701 million, up 47% year-over-year
- **Free Cash Flow:** €834 million for the quarter, €2.9 billion for the full year

*Translation: Spotify made significantly more money than anyone expected, across every major financial measure. Free cash flow — the actual cash the business generates after paying its bills — hit €2.9 billion for the year, which tells you this isn't just an accounting trick. Real money is coming in.*

## Premium: The Engine of the Business

Premium subscription revenue grew 8% to €4.01 billion. This segment accounts for the vast majority of Spotify's total revenue.

Spotify has raised prices multiple times — in 2023, 2024, and again in early 2026 (US Premium is now $12.99/month). Each time, people predicted a wave of cancellations. It hasn't happened. Churn remains historically low.

*Translation: "Churn" is the percentage of paying subscribers who cancel each month. Low churn after a price increase means people consider Spotify essential enough to keep paying more for it. That's called "pricing power" — and it's one of the most valuable things a company can have.*

The company also launched "Music Pro" (sometimes called "Supremium"), a higher tier at an extra $5.99/month offering lossless audio, exclusive artist Q&As, and early merch access. It's designed to get more money from the most dedicated users without pushing casual listeners away.

## Users: Three Quarters of a Billion People

Spotify added 38 million monthly active users in Q4 — the most it's ever added in a single quarter. That brings the total to **751 million MAUs**, which is 11% higher than a year ago and 6 million above their own guidance.

Within that number, **290 million are paying subscribers** (up 10%), and 476 million are on the free, ad-supported tier (up 12%).

Most of the growth came from Latin America and "Rest of World" markets. But here's the thing that matters for revenue: North America and Europe still make up 61% of all paying subscribers. Those are the markets where Spotify charges the most.

*Translation: MAU stands for "monthly active users" — anyone who opened the app at least once that month. The number that matters more is paying subscribers (290 million), because those are the people generating steady, predictable revenue.*

## Advertising: The Weak Spot

Not everything was perfect. Ad revenue dropped 4% year-over-year to €518 million. On a constant-currency basis it was up 4%, but the headline number is still a decline.

*Translation: "Constant currency" means stripping out the effect of exchange rate changes between currencies. Since Spotify reports in euros but earns money globally, currency swings can make growth look worse (or better) than it really is. The underlying ad business grew slightly, but the reported number still fell.*

Spotify is betting on its "Spotify Ad Exchange" (SAX) platform to turn this around — essentially letting advertisers buy audio and video ads programmatically, the way they do on Google or Meta. It's early days, but this is where the next leg of growth could come from.

## What's Changed: From Growth-at-All-Costs to Profitable Machine

For most of its life, Spotify was a growth story. The company burned cash trying to sign up as many users as possible, competing with Apple Music, Amazon Music, YouTube Music, and everyone else.

That changed in 2023-2024. Spotify cut jobs, tightened spending on podcasts, and focused on making the business actually profitable. It worked — dramatically. Operating margin went from basically nothing to 15.5% in Q4.

*Translation: Operating margin tells you how much profit a company makes from each pound or dollar of revenue after paying its day-to-day costs. A 15.5% operating margin means for every €100 Spotify earns, it keeps €15.50 as profit. Two years ago, that number was close to zero.*

Founder Daniel Ek stepped back to Executive Chairman on January 1, 2026, handing day-to-day leadership to co-CEOs Alex Norström and Gustav Söderström. Norström called 2026 the "Year of Raising Ambition."

## Beyond Music: Audiobooks, Video, and AI

Spotify isn't just a music app anymore. Here's what they're building:

**Audiobooks:** Now available in multiple markets. 25% of Premium subscribers have engaged with audiobooks. This is Spotify trying to take a piece of Audible's business.

**Video Podcasts:** Music videos in beta are now live in 111 markets for Premium users. The platform hosts over 530,000 video podcast shows. More time on the app means it's harder to cancel.

**AI Features:** Spotify launched "Prompted Playlist" (AI-generated playlists based on your prompts) and is investing heavily in AI-powered discovery. Co-CEO Söderström said the company sees itself as "the R&D department for the music industry" when it comes to AI.

**Spotify Wrapped 2025:** Over 300 million users engaged, generating 630 million social media shares. This isn't just a marketing campaign — it's a moat.

*Translation: A "moat" in investing means a competitive advantage that's hard for rivals to copy. The more Spotify knows about your listening habits, the better its recommendations get, and the harder it is for you to leave for Apple Music or YouTube Music. Wrapped — where you see your personalised year in music — reinforces that lock-in every December.*

## What's Coming in 2026

Spotify's guidance for Q1 2026:

- **MAUs:** 759 million (up 8 million from Q4)
- **Premium Subscribers:** 293 million
- **Revenue:** €4.5 billion
- **Gross Margin:** 32.8%
- **Operating Income:** €660 million

The numbers suggest steady growth and stable margins. Not a huge acceleration, but not a slowdown either.

The company also spent $433 million buying back its own shares in Q4 ($510 million for the full year).

*Translation: When a company buys back its own shares, it reduces the total number of shares available. That means each remaining share represents a bigger slice of the company's profits. It's a way of returning value to shareholders without paying a dividend.*

## The Bottom Line for Investors

The bear case on Spotify was always: "It's just a middleman between record labels and listeners, and it'll never make real money." Q4 2025 answers that decisively. Gross margins above 33%, operating margins above 15%, €2.9 billion in free cash flow for the year — this is a profitable business.

The bull case is that Spotify is becoming the default "audio utility" for three quarters of a billion people, with expanding pricing power, new revenue streams (audiobooks, video, ad exchange), and AI features that deepen the moat.

The risk? Ad revenue is still weak. Competition from Apple, Amazon, and YouTube isn't going anywhere. And at some point, price hikes do have a ceiling.
